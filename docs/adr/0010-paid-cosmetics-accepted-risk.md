# Paid cosmetics ship, as an accepted and revocable risk

ash will sell cosmetic entitlements despite a plain-text conflict with two independent clauses — the Usage Guidelines' bar on a mod verifying out-of-game ownership that affects in-game features, and the EULA's "don't sell them for money" line on mods. This is a deliberate choice made with the conflict understood, on the same footing Lunar Client and Badlion have operated on for years, not an oversight or an assumption that the terms permit it.

## What this decision is not

It is not a finding that the model complies. Mojang declines to pre-clear specific projects and directs anyone unsure to an attorney. Lunar's and Badlion's continued operation is evidence of non-enforcement, not of permission — their own terms assert non-affiliation and claim no compliance. Nothing here has been reviewed by a lawyer.

## Consequences

- **The position is revocable by Mojang at any time, without notice.** Cosmetics revenue must not be the only thing keeping the project alive, and the cosmetics system should be severable — if it has to be switched off, the launcher and client must remain functional without it.
- **Scale asymmetry is real.** An established client is expensive to enforce against and has a relationship to protect. A new entrant has neither. Do not reason from "they get away with it" to "we will."
- The compliance surface is copied deliberately rather than invented: a named legal entity as publisher and seller; a prominent non-affiliation disclaimer; cosmetics licensed, never sold as owned property; any wallet currency carrying no cash value, non-transferable, with no cash-out path.
- Keep this decision separate from the Java Edition API allow-list application. Do not put a monetisation question in front of the security review that gates API access.
- Revisit if Mojang's terms change, if enforcement activity against comparable clients appears, or before taking outside investment — a revocable core revenue stream is a diligence question.
